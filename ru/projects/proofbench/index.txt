The agent is qwen3.5:9b running locally on one GPU. On the baseline it was fully correct on 75.0% of attempts and did nothing dangerous on 95.8%. An inference server can run only a few requests at once and queues the rest out of sight, where nothing can reorder or refuse them; a plain load balancer spreads that overload evenly and hides it everywhere. Switchyard keeps the queue in front of the servers, sends each replica only as many requests as it has slots, and refuses what cannot be served in time with a retry time. It is written in Go with no dependencies outside the standard library.

Measured on one RTX A4000 serving qwen3.5:9b through two replicas, Ollama and llama.cpp on the same weights, against a plain round-robin proxy fed identical open-loop chat traffic: at 1.0 requests a second the proxy got 6 of 212 requests a first token within 10 seconds and 150 failed; Switchyard got 78, failed none, and refused the rest in at most 3 ms. Routing conversations back to their cache made no measurable difference at this scale, and the write-up says so.
