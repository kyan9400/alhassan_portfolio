The stack is a React 18 + TypeScript single-page app (Vite, Tailwind CSS), a Node.js / Express 5 REST API and MongoDB (Mongoose). The core is the violations and fines module: server-side filtering by name, department, type, status, depot and period, pagination, photo and video attachments, and deletion confirmed by password. Around it sit modules for employees, driver call-backs, a document archive on Cloudinary with a local-disk fallback, and in-app notifications. Sign-in uses JWT (bcrypt), by email or phone.

The home page shows KPI tiles per depot, and violations, employees, driver calls and the depot summary export to Excel right in the browser. Signatures are verified against the exact captured body for GitHub, Stripe (with its five-minute receipt window) and generic HMAC-SHA-256 profiles; the secret is used once and never stored. Captured requests can be replayed to an endpoint of your choice, but conservatively: private, loopback and non-HTTP targets are rejected, DNS is re-checked on connect, redirects are limited, and authorization, cookie and hop-by-hop headers are stripped.

It keeps a bounded event history in a local JSON snapshot, preserves binary bodies as base64 and exposes a health probe. From April to October 2026, under civil-law contracts, I built a search service over it: an engineer types a cable mark, a document code, an equipment designation or a phrase and gets the document, the PDF page (or Excel sheet and rows, Word section), a text fragment and the network path. Documents never leave the company network.

Search is hybrid, with no LLM generation: a custom inverted index over words and designations, plus FAISS semantic search on sentence-transformers embeddings (all-MiniLM-L6-v2). Designations are matched by general rules: look-alike Latin and Cyrillic letters, spaces and hyphens do not break a match, while numbers and marks are never substituted. Scans are detected automatically and selected pages go through Tesseract + Poppler OCR. The backend is FastAPI, the interface is plain HTML/JS, and it runs as a systemd service on Ubuntu Server with an atomic index swap and rollback.

Code and mark queries went from 9–10 s to 1.2–1.7 s and phrase queries from 55–70 s to 2–4 s; after the move to the inverted index, p95 is 0.18 s on 55 control queries. Quality is held by 739 pytest tests and a 104-query control set with top-1/top-5 metrics. I built it with AI agents (Codex, Cursor, Claude Code): I gathered the requirements, wrote the agent briefs, reviewed the changes and checked them on the production index.
