3:T4ab,Lumen Search is an open-source prototype I built with AI agents, on the same theme as my document-search work: one search over PDFs, Word files, Excel sheets and scans, where every answer shows exactly where it came from. It indexes eight public documents — NIST and OWASP security guidance in English, the Russian personal data law in Russian, and two scans that only OCR can read.

Parsing follows each document's structure — PDF bookmarks and numbered headings, Word heading styles, one spreadsheet row per record — so results cite a page and section, an article of a law, or a sheet and row. Retrieval runs BM25 over Snowball-stemmed English and Russian words next to multilingual-e5 embeddings in FAISS, fuses the two rankings, and can rerank the top results with a multilingual cross-encoder.

Quality is measured on 62 hand-labelled questions: hybrid search puts a correct passage in the top five for 75.8% of them, and reranking raises that to 83.9%; the README also says where it is weak. From April to October 2026, under civil-law contracts, I built a search service over it: an engineer types a cable mark, a document code, an equipment designation or a phrase and gets the document, the PDF page (or Excel sheet and rows, Word section), a text fragment and the network path. Documents never leave the company network.

Search is hybrid, with no LLM generation: a custom inverted index over words and designations, plus FAISS semantic search on sentence-transformers embeddings (all-MiniLM-L6-v2). Designations are matched by general rules: look-alike Latin and Cyrillic letters, spaces and hyphens do not break a match, while numbers and marks are never substituted. Scans are detected automatically and selected pages go through Tesseract + Poppler OCR. The backend is FastAPI, the interface is plain HTML/JS, and it runs as a systemd service on Ubuntu Server with an atomic index swap and rollback.

Code and mark queries went from 9–10 s to 1.2–1.7 s and phrase queries from 55–70 s to 2–4 s; after the move to the inverted index, p95 is 0.18 s on 55 control queries. Quality is held by 739 pytest tests and a 104-query control set with top-1/top-5 metrics. I built it with AI agents (Codex, Cursor, Claude Code): I gathered the requirements, wrote the agent briefs, reviewed the changes and checked them on the production index.
