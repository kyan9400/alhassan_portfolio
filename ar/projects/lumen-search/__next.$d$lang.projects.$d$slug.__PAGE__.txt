3:T4ab,Lumen Search is an open-source prototype I built with AI agents, on the same theme as my document-search work: one search over PDFs, Word files, Excel sheets and scans, where every answer shows exactly where it came from. It indexes eight public documents — NIST and OWASP security guidance in English, the Russian personal data law in Russian, and two scans that only OCR can read.

Parsing follows each document's structure — PDF bookmarks and numbered headings, Word heading styles, one spreadsheet row per record — so results cite a page and section, an article of a law, or a sheet and row. Retrieval runs BM25 over Snowball-stemmed English and Russian words next to multilingual-e5 embeddings in FAISS, fuses the two rankings, and can rerank the top results with a multilingual cross-encoder.

Quality is measured on 62 hand-labelled questions: hybrid search puts a correct passage in the top five for 75.8% of them, and reranking raises that to 83.9%; the README also says where it is weak. The backend is Python: CVs in PDF, Word or scans go through Tesseract OCR into a profile with dated roles and evidence for each skill, pgvector embeddings power hybrid search, and a C++ skill matcher has a pure-Python fallback.
